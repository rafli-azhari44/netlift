# T04: Users who bought with free shipping and never came back

**From:** Bu Ayu, PM D2C, Friday 15:20
**Request:** "Bisa kasih data user yang beli karena gratis ongkir tapi nggak pernah beli lagi setelahnya? Buat riset retensi."

## The real question

Bu Ayu wants to know whether the free-shipping offer brought in lasting customers or one-time deal seekers. That is a good question, but the list she asked for cannot answer it honestly today, for two reasons: the follow-up window is too short, and the data holds almost no repeat purchases from experiment customers yet. The useful version compares repeat purchase between variant B and variant A converters, once every customer has had the same, long enough window.

## Assumptions I made

- "Beli karena gratis ongkir" = converted in variant B. The data cannot show one person's motivation; variant A shows what would have happened without the offer.
- "Riset retensi" needs a comparison, and possibly people to interview, not a full customer list.
- Converters include registered customers (IDs starting with C) and first-time visitors without an account (IDs starting with V). Visitor IDs stay the same throughout the experiment, so they can be followed and have to be included: they are 87% of converters (3,683 of 4,244).

## Answer

> Bu Ayu, happy to help. I'd like to adjust the question so the answer holds up.
>
> **Why not "never bought again":** our data stops on 30 June, and the experiment ran until 14 June. Someone who first ordered on 12 June has had less than three weeks to come back. Calling them churned would mix real churners with people we have not watched long enough.
>
> **What the data shows today:** almost no repeat purchases from experiment converters yet. None of the 3,683 first-time visitors who converted has ordered again, and the first repeat orders from registered customers only appear from 22 June (72 of 561). A two-week repeat comparison would read 0.0% for A and 0.2% for B, which is too thin to say anything about either variant. That level is also far below what our registered customers normally do (about three orders each over six months), so before relying on it I want to confirm with data engineering that follow-up orders from experiment customers are being captured.
>
> **What I suggest:** I set up the B-versus-A repeat-purchase comparison now and run it on data through the end of August, with a 60-day window for every customer and first-time visitors included. If B's repeat rate comes out clearly lower than A's, the offer attracted more one-time buyers.
>
> **If you need people to interview sooner:** I can share a small sample of registered customers who converted in variant B, with pseudonymised IDs that your team can match in the CRM, instead of a full list. That keeps the request in line with the limitation and purpose principles of UU PDP No. 27/2022.

## Limitations

- A 60-day window measures medium-term repeat purchase, not long-term loyalty.
- Guest checkouts (IDs starting with G) get a new ID on every order and cannot be followed; none of them are in the experiment population.
- First-time visitors cannot be interviewed through the CRM because they have no account, so an interview sample covers registered customers only.
- Variant B's group is skewed by the Android 6.4.0 bucketing bug, so the comparison will also be checked per device.
- "Bought because of free shipping" can only be estimated for the group (B versus A), never for one person.
- Portfolio note: in this synthetic dataset, first-time visitors never place a second order, and registered customers who were in the experiment have no other orders until 22 June. The near-zero repeat rate reflects how the data was generated, not customer behaviour.
